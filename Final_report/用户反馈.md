### Paper summary - keep it short (Your summary of what the paper is about)
This paper is about GrayPulse, a latency-aware mitigation framework for detecting and suppressing gray-failure impact. GrayPulse aims to detect sustained performance degradation rather than one-off spikes. In this report, the authors proposed a tentative design and a preliminary experiment, arguing that gray failure has a measurable and complicated impact on distributed inference systems.

### Strengths (a few bullets)
- GrayPulse occupies a distinct point in the design spectrum, compared to the timeout-only, request-count-only, and gateway-only approaches.
- Clearly identified the issue of tail-explosion caused by gray failures, and treated it as a robust, hysteresis-aware control problem, which addresses the system's stability under noisy degradation.
- The preliminary experiment is comprehensive and highlights the non-uniformity of gray-failure's impact on different models, baselines, and gateways.

### Ways the Paper can Improve (a few bullets). Be gentle, but also honest.
- The mathematical foundation of GrayPulse is yet unclear. The formulas, variables, and estimation methods under section III are not well-explained or referenced, leading to confusion.
- There is a lack of in-line citations throughout the report. It is hard to identify the trace of claims or prior research results referenced in the report.
- The preliminary experiment results identified many challenges in optimizing both gateway and routing efficiency. However, the paper didn't mention how GrayPulse would address these issues.

### Detailed Comments (give detailed feedback, justify your ratings, and your bullets above). Be gentle, but also honest. Write as much as you want. Justify all your scores, and avoid writing short reviews.
This report shows that the proposed project, GrayPulse, aims to solve a real and impactful issue. First of all, it clearly states the problem of gray failure in distributed DNN inference systems and points out several challenges in addressing this problem. Second, the impact of gray faults is supported by the preliminary experiment results. In the experiments, inference was conducted on 2 workloads, ResNet-50 and MobileBERT. 3 different existing gray fault defenses were evaluated, including Round-Robin, TRi-CB, and P2C-PEWMA. The experiment results display the impact of gray failures as well as the varied abilities of different defenses in mitigating these failures.

However, the proposed solution, GrayPulse, is yet under a pretty immature stage of formulation and development. In section III, the authors introduced the goal and mathematical model of GrayPulse, but didn't connect its design with the problem that it aims to solve. This section briefly touched on the control signals and thresholds in GrayPulse's model, like the dispersion estimate *MADi*, signal zi threshold theta_high and theta_low then, it didn't cover the problem of how these signals, estimations, or thresholds are obtained, and how they help GrayPulse in achieving its goals. Also, the lack of inline citations makes it hard to identify if this proposed mathematical model is developed from any existing works.

The reviewer would recommend that the authors put more effort into formulating the model of GrayPulse, logically proving the effectiveness of the model, or supporting this model by referencing previous pieces of literature on this subject.

### Paper summary - keep it short (Your summary of what the paper is about)
This paper studies gray failures in distributed inference systems and proposes **GrayPulse**, a control-plane, latency-aware mitigation framework that uses robust latency statistics and hysteresis-based routing control to detect and suppress fail-slow backends. The midterm report mainly establishes the problem setting, presents the system design, and provides preliminary baseline experiments on ResNet-50 and MobileBERT workloads under injected gray failures.

### Strengths (a few bullets)
- The paper tackles a practical and important problem. Gray failures in distributed inference are easy to overlook but can have a big impact on tail latency, so I think the motivation is strong and well explained.
- I also liked that the proposed system is fairly concrete rather than just conceptual. The report gives a clear design intuition for GrayPulse and already includes some preliminary experimental results, which makes the project feel grounded and feasible.
- Another strength is that the paper seems aware of tradeoffs instead of overselling the idea. It is trying to improve latency under gray failures while staying practical for real inference deployments, which gives the work a nice systems angle.

### Ways the Paper can Improve (a few bullets). Be gentle, but also honest.
- The main idea is promising, but the central hypothesis and claimed contribution could be stated a bit more sharply up front. Right now I can understand the general motivation, but the "one-sentence takeaway" of exactly what GrayPulse does better than prior approaches could be easier to identify.
- Since this is a midterm report, I think the biggest thing to strengthen is the experimental section. The paper already has some preliminary results, which is good, but it would be more convincing with clearer comparisons, more discussion of gains vs. tradeoffs, and a more direct connection from the data back to the stated hypothesis.
- I would also like to see a bit more detail on the limitations or potential downsides of the approach. For example, if GrayPulse improves tail latency by being more aggressive in routing or mitigation, it would help to discuss what it might cost in terms of overhead, false positives, or stability under different workloads.
- The system design is fairly concrete already, but some parts of the evaluation setup still feel a little early-stage. Adding more detail on the baselines, failure scenarios, and why those scenarios are representative would make the report feel more complete and better aligned with the project goals for the midterm stage.

### Detailed Comments (give detailed feedback, justify your ratings, and your bullets above). Be gentle, but also honest. Write as much as you want. Justify all your scores, and avoid writing short reviews.
I think this is a solid midterm report overall. The project problem is meaningful, and the paper does a good job motivating why gray failures matter in distributed inference systems. The proposed direction also feels practical rather than overly abstract, which made the report easier to follow. From a midterm-report perspective, I also appreciate that the paper is not just presenting an idea in the abstract — it already has an initial system design and some preliminary experimental data, which is important for this stage.

What worked best for me was that the paper already has a reasonably clear systems angle. It is trying to detect or mitigate fail-slow behavior in a way that connects directly to latency-sensitive inference serving, so the project feels relevant and well motivated. The report also seems to be thinking about evaluation early, instead of postponing all experiments to the final report, and I think that matches the expectations for the midterm pretty well.

At the same time, I think the report could still improve in a few places. The biggest one is that the core hypothesis and main claimed contribution could be stated more sharply. After reading, I understand the general goal, but the paper's main "takeaway sentence" is still a little diffuse. Since the midterm instructions emphasize clearly stating the hypothesis, techniques, gains, losses, and tradeoffs, I think the paper would benefit from making those points more explicit and easier to identify.

I also think the experiments are the main place where the next version can become much stronger. It is good that there are already preliminary results, but right now I wanted a more direct link between the results and the paper's claims. For example, it would help to show more clearly what baseline the method is being compared against, what exact gain is being achieved, and what the cost or tradeoff is. Even a small but well-explained set of comparisons would make the argument much more convincing.

Related to that, I would have liked a bit more discussion of limitations. The report mainly explains why the approach should help, but for a systems paper it would also be useful to say where it might not work as well, or what overheads it introduces. That would make the paper feel more balanced and would help connect back to the "what do your techniques lose you?" part of the midterm requirements.

Overall, I think the project is on a good track. The topic is interesting, the problem matters, and there is enough implementation/evaluation progress already that the paper feels like a real systems project rather than just a proposal. My main suggestion is to tighten the central claim and make the experimental story more explicit around gains, tradeoffs, and support for the hypothesis.

### Paper summary - keep it short (Your summary of what the paper is about)
This paper claims gray failures are a large, yet subtle, contributor towards the impact of failures in distributed systems. They support this with experimental results which show that among various load-balancing policies for DNN inference, gray failures contribute to large increases in latency, particularly 'tail-explosions'. The authors also describe the system model for a novel failure-detector/load-balancer known as GrayPulse. GrayPulse uses the previous healthy history of a device as a baseline, and is able to detect deviations from the median, which might point towards potential gray-failures. In the future they hope to compare GrayPulse against current implementations to see its potential benefit towards failure mitigation.

### Strengths (a few bullets)
- Clear scope, focus, and future direction. This paper serves mainly as motivation for more research in the area of gray failures, rather than showing results for a not-yet-complete implementation of GrayPulse.
- Using the healthy history of a device to detect gray failures is a really interesting approach. I also think leveraging the behavior of gray failures, particularly tail latency, strengthens your report.
- You analyze a lot of experimental results, which does a lot to support the claim that gray failures are a dominant failure mode. I also think the experiments make sense contextually, that is, you chose the right baselines, parameters, and metrics.

### Ways the Paper can Improve (a few bullets). Be gentle, but also honest.
- I understand that for current baselines, there issues which arise due to gray failures. I am still unclear as to why GrayPulse is uniquely able to mitigate these issues. You claim that is it not clear yet which implementation is superior; however, I think GrayPulse should have a defining characteristic which, at least theoretically, should show it fills a gap. I think experimental and theoretical superiority are two separate entities.
- I think you should go more into detail on how GrayPulse is tail-aware. Sustained degradation and tail explosion affect the average in (I assume) different ways.

### Detailed Comments (give detailed feedback, justify your ratings, and your bullets above). Be gentle, but also honest. Write as much as you want. Justify all your scores, and avoid writing short reviews.

## Design
- [Section II] The failure model includes information that would be more relevant to mention a second time in section IV, specifically, the 'stress-ng' command.
- [Section III] Must GrayPulse be implemented on healthy hardware to be useful? Suppose the system is heterogeneous, and GrayPulse is used on an already degraded device. How would one be able to distinguish between a gray failure and naturally slower hardware? If there is no baseline to work off of, then would GrayPulse only be able to detect further degradation?
- [Section III] A really strong aspect of your design is multiple penalization states. Reduces false-positives, but simultaneously prepares recovery early if necessary.
